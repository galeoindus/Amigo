# Global Crisis & Helpline Directory

Amigo uses this file to give a user the right crisis, mental-health, child-protection or domestic-violence number for their own country. It covers every region of the world and is written to be self-contained, so it can be downloaded and used on its own.

> **Read this first.** Helpline numbers, hours and coverage change, and some listed lines may have been discontinued since this directory was compiled (October 2026). In any immediate, life-threatening emergency, always dial the **local general emergency number** first. If a country's crisis line is marked **unverified**, or is not listed, point the user to a global directory (below) rather than guessing. **Never invent a helpline number.**

## How Amigo should use this directory

1. Identify the user's country if it is known or can be safely inferred; if unsure, ask briefly or share the global directories below.
2. In immediate danger, give the **Emergency** number first, then a crisis line.
3. Match the line to the need: mental-health/suicide crisis, a child in distress, or domestic violence.
4. Prefer the local number. If the country is not listed or shows "unverified", direct the user to Find a Helpline or Befrienders Worldwide.
5. Share calmly and briefly; do not overwhelm. Never promise secrecy in imminent danger.

## Global directories (work for most countries)

- **Find a Helpline** — https://findahelpline.com — free emotional-support lines in 130+ countries, filterable by country and topic.
- **Befrienders Worldwide** — https://befrienders.org — suicide-prevention centres worldwide.
- **IASP crisis-centre directory** — https://www.iasp.info/resources/Crisis_Centres/
- **Child Helpline International** — https://childhelplineinternational.org/helplines/
- **WHO mental health** — https://www.who.int/health-topics/mental-health
- **EU**: single emergency number **112**; **116 111** child helplines; **116 006** victim support; **116 016** women's violence; **116 000** missing children.
- **United States & Canada**: **988** Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
- **India**: emergency **112**; Tele-MANAS **14416 / 1800-891-4416**; Women **181**; Childline **1098**.

## Coverage and gaps

- Countries covered: 47 (the sovereign states of Europe plus Kosovo and Vatican City, including Russia and Turkey).
- Micro-states (Andorra, Monaco, San Marino, Liechtenstein, Vatican City) have few dedicated national lines; several cells are marked unverified.
- No confirmed dedicated national suicide/crisis line was found for: Albania, Monaco, San Marino, Turkey, Ukraine (Lifeline Ukraine 7333 currently paused), Vatican City. Bulgaria's entry is the Bulgarian Red Cross psychosocial line (not suicide-specific).
- No confirmed child helpline was found for: Belarus, Kosovo, Monaco, Montenegro, San Marino, Vatican City.
- No confirmed women's/DV helpline was found for: Belarus, Kosovo, Vatican City.

Sources used per row are listed in the final column (findahelpline.com country pages, the Council of Europe Istanbul Convention helpline directory, the official EU 112 page, and national government/emergency-service pages).

## Notes, scope and verification

- **Emergency numbers** are general public-safety numbers (police/ambulance/fire or a single unified number). Where a country has no single unified number, the main police/ambulance/fire codes are shown. Source: the compilation *List of emergency telephone numbers* (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_emergency_telephone_numbers), which itself cites national police/government pages.
- **Primary helpline sources used:** findahelpline.com (ThroughLine/IASP partner directory), national government and health-ministry pages (health.gov.bb, gov.gd, mujer.gov.py, app.mujer.gob.pa, health.gov.vc, pressoffice.gov.bz, canada.ca, jis.gov.jm), Child Helpline International, and PAHO-adjacent Caribbean MHPSS directories (healthycaribbean.org). The compilation *List of suicide crisis lines* (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_suicide_crisis_lines) was used as a cross-check and as the sole source for a small number of entries.
- **"unverified"** means no number could be confirmed against an authoritative source at the time of compilation — it is a deliberate gap, not a missing field. This is most common for dedicated **child** and **women's/DV** lines in smaller Caribbean and Central American states, where only a government department (not a published hotline) exists.
- **Emergency vs. crisis:** in an immediate danger to life, dial the Emergency number. Crisis/mental-health lines are for emotional distress and suicidal thoughts; they are not emergency dispatch services.
- **Changing information:** helpline numbers change. Grenada's 988 line was launched in July 2026; Barbados' Lifeline Barbados is a newer Ministry-of-Health service. Re-check before relying on any single number for a high-stakes situation.

## Notes on coverage

- **Best-covered countries** (multiple confirmed lines): South Africa, Kenya, Nigeria, Uganda, Botswana, Namibia, Malawi, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Tanzania, Egypt, Israel, Lebanon, Jordan, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Iran.
- **Countries where no national suicide/crisis line could be confirmed** ("unverified"): Angola, Cameroon, CAR, Chad, Comoros, Congo (Rep.), DRC, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Ghana (dedicated suicide line exists but not confirmed), Guinea-Bissau, Libya, Mali, Niger, São Tomé and Príncipe, Tunisia, and several Middle East states (Bahrain, Iraq, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Syria, Yemen). Many of these have no dedicated mental-health line at all; in a crisis, call the emergency number or the nearest hospital.
- **Pan-African child line:** the short code **116** is the harmonised African child helpline number and works in many of the countries above.
- **Numbers to double-check before publication:** entries marked "(number unverified)" and all lines sourced from news or secondary compilations rather than a helpline/government page. Numbers also change frequently; re-verify against findahelpline.com, Befrienders Worldwide and the relevant ministry before publishing.
