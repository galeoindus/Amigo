# Amigo Presentation Design Ideas

## Before starting

- Identify the deck's audience and what they need to understand or decide.
- Use the Amigo palette and supplied brand assets as defaults, not as a substitute for a user's own brand rules.
- Let one color dominate the visual hierarchy; use one or two supporting tones and a limited accent.
- Prefer light content slides and a strong cover/closing slide when appropriate. A dark slide is acceptable when it improves the narrative and maintains contrast.

## Layout patterns

- Two-column: explanation plus illustration or evidence.
- Icon and text rows: small symbol, clear claim, concise explanation.
- 2×2 or 2×3 grid: compare a few related options without overloading them.
- Before/after or comparison columns: use shared scales and comparable criteria.
- Timeline or process flow: number steps and clearly show direction.
- Chart plus takeaway: chart is evidence; headline is the interpretation.
- Large metric callout: include the unit, time period, population/denominator, and source where relevant.

## Spacing and hierarchy

Use consistent margins, consistent gaps, and enough breathing room. Body copy is usually left-aligned. Center only titles or intentionally short labels. Prefer a short headline that states a clear message. Avoid shrinking the font to squeeze too much material onto a slide; edit or split the content.

## Avoid

- 12 unrelated charts or a slide with more than one main idea.
- Repeating the same layout on every slide.
- Decorative lines under every heading, excessive icons, fake 3D, and unnecessary animations.
- Using color as the only signal or using light text on low-contrast backgrounds.
- Unlabeled axes, omitted units, ambiguous legends, unsourced facts, or unmarked illustrative numbers.
- Treating a screenshot of a chart as a substitute for a native editable chart when the source data is available.

## Slide-by-slide check

Every slide should do at least one useful job: orient, explain, compare, prove, warn, recommend, or close. Add imagery only when it clarifies an idea. Check the slide at the size it will actually be viewed.
